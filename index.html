Section 01 // Aerodynamic Excellence

Post-Stall Supermaneuverability & Combat Aerobatics

Deep mechanical explanation of how Su-35S defies traditional aerodynamic stalls using 3D TVC and fly-by-wire thrust augmentation.

What is Post-Stall Flight?

Conventional fighter jets rely strictly on air passing over their wings to maintain lift and control. Once airflow separates (stall angle exceeded, usually above 30° AoA), conventional controls fail.

The Su-35S uses 3D Thrust Vectoring Control (TVC) to direct the engine thrust plume itself, allowing full directional control even at 0 km/h airspeed and angles of attack reaching 180°.

Tactical Advantage in Dogfights

In Close-In Combat (WVR - Within Visual Range), supermaneuverability allows the pilot to instantaneously point the aircraft’s nose (and infrared missiles/30mm cannon) towards an enemy fighter without changing the flight path vector.

This rapid off-boresight nose alignment combined with High-Off-Boresight (HOBS) missiles like the R-74M gives the Su-35S unmatched close-quarters lethal capacity.

Signature Maneuvers & Flight Mechanics

MANEUVER 01

Pugachev's Cobra (Кобра)

The aircraft pitches back rapidly to a pitch angle between 110°–120° without gaining altitude, braking horizontally before dropping back to level flight.

Speed Range: 300 - 450 km/h
Max AoA: >110°
MANEUVER 02

Frolov Chakra (Kulbit)

An extremely tight 360-degree loop executed within a turning radius no wider than the aircraft’s own length, stalling the jet entirely inside the loop.

Turn Radius: Near Zero
AoA: 360° Continuous
MANEUVER 03

Helicopter Flat Spin (Pancake)

The aircraft halts horizontal velocity, stands on its tail, and executes a full 360° horizontal rotation like a helicopter rotor before accelerating away.

Forward Speed: ~0 km/h
Rotation Axis: Vertical Yaw
MANEUVER 04

Herbster Maneuver

Rapid pitch-up into post-stall followed by an immediate asymmetric TVC yaw deflection, allowing rapid direction changes without drifting wide.

Tactical Use: Missile Avoidance
Deceleration: Instantaneous

Airframe Engineering & Composite Materials

Extensive titanium integration providing high-G structural endurance without additional weight.

Titanium Spars & Center Fuselage

Heavy load-bearing wing carry-through beams and central fuselage joints utilize forged high-strength titanium alloys, extending total airframe operational life to 4,000 flight hours.

02 / Canard Removal

Clean Aerodynamic Surface

Removal of front canards (present on older Su-33/37 variants) reduces weight, aerodynamic drag, and radar signature while relying completely on software-driven FBW tail surfaces.

Intake ducts are coated with Radar-Absorbing Materials (RAM) and feature radar-blocking engine face screens, lowering frontal RCS down to 1.0 - 3.0 m².

Section 03 // Propulsion Systems

Saturn AL-41F1S (Item 117S) Engines

Twin turbofans equipped with fully independent 3D omnidirectional vectoring nozzles.

The AL-41F1S is a deep modernization of the AL-31FP engine, featuring a larger fan diameter (932 mm vs 905 mm), modern low-pressure turbine blades, and an advanced digital engine control system (FADEC).

Dry Thrust (Military Power) 8,800 kgf (86.3 kN) per engine

Cruising thrust without engaging afterburners.

Full Afterburner Thrust 14,500 kgf (142.2 kN) per engine

Emergency thrust for combat maneuvers & rapid climb.

3D TVC Nozzle Deflection Speed 30° per second

Hydraulic actuators rotate nozzles independently in pitch and yaw axes.

TVC Nozzle Axis Configuration

The nozzle rotational axes are canted at a 32-degree angle outwards. This allows symmetrical pitching, differential rolling, and yawing control simply by manipulating the twin nozzle angles.

Pitch Deflection: ±15° (Up / Down)
Yaw Deflection: ±15° (Left / Right)
Time Between Overhaul (TBO): 1,000 Flight Hours
Total Engine Service Life: 4,000 Hours

Section 05 // Human-Machine Interface

Glass Cockpit Architecture

Full digital glass cockpit eliminating analog gauges for situational awareness.

Dual MFI-55 Multi-Function Displays

Two massive 15-inch color LCD displays (1400x1050 pixels) with picture-in-picture capability, rendering tactical maps, weapons menus, radar scans, and engine parameters simultaneously.

IKSh-1M Wide-Angle HUD

Collimated head-up display projecting velocity vectors, pitch lines, weapon target boxes, and flight guidance directly onto the windshield glass.

Sura-M Helmet Mounted Sight (HMS)

Allows the pilot to lock onto enemy aircraft simply by looking at them through the helmet visor, turning the missile seeker head up to 60° off the aircraft's center line.
